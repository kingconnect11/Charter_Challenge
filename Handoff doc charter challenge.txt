Handoff: Features To Build Next
Strategic Vision
The next leg is about turning a good prototype into a living charter business simulator with real operational tension. The goal is to deepen economics, make time and geography matter, and create memorable stories from risk, logistics, and people decisions. What follows is only the new work that is not in the current build summary. Treat this as the blueprint for the next sprints.
Dynamic Charter Market and Demand Model
Build a location and season driven marketplace rather than static offers. Each region gets an annual demand curve with weekly variance, holiday spikes, and weather sensitivity. A rainy week in the islands suppresses luxury demand and boosts transport and reposition jobs. Reputation and recent reviews should modulate both price and frequency of high tier requests. Add corporate events, regattas, film shoots, and festival charters on specific dates to create scramble moments. When the fleet is idle, the system should present low paying filler work that keeps cash flowing while telegraphing better work if you wait or position elsewhere.
Example: late June in the San Juans generates a cluster of family week charters with a clear price ladder and a rival operator occasionally sniping the best request if you hesitate.
Real Vessel Performance Curves
Replace single speed and single range with a performance envelope. Model fuel burn as a cubic function of speed with an idle and hotel load floor. Add sea state penalties that reduce comfortable cruise and increase burn. Let the player choose a speed setpoint per leg that trades time for cost. Add fuel capacity, reserve policy, and a safety buffer that can be tuned per captain.
Example: a Nordhavn at nine knots burns modestly but arrives a day later than at eleven; the faster run shortens cleaning layover and makes the next high value charter possible, which flips the optimal choice.
Fuel, Water, and Waste Logistics
Introduce real bunkering and water maker concepts. Each port exposes fuel price, truck availability, and timing constraints. Add fresh water, grey water, and black water limits for small ports so that long periods at anchor require either marina touch and go or water production downtime. Route planning should warn for insufficient range or waste capacity and suggest a pit stop with time and cost.
Spares, Breakdowns, and Lead Times
Create a light parts inventory system linked to reliability. Carrying critical spares reduces downtime after common failures. Skipping spares saves weight and money but increases the risk of multi day delays waiting for a courier to reach a remote island. Incidents should cite concrete parts by name such as pump impeller or starter motor so the loop feels tangible.
Insurance, Claims, and Risk Pricing
Add an insurance policy with tiers. Higher premiums lower catastrophic loss exposure and speed claim payouts. Poor maintenance history increases premiums. Certain routes and seasons get surcharges. After an incident, a claim investigation may tie up cash and reputation for a week.
Example: you take an early season trip through a gale prone passage and pay a short term storm rider that keeps the bank comfortable and unlocks a higher loan ceiling.
Regulatory and Paperwork Simulation
Create a compliance checklist per voyage. Immigration preclearance, cruising permits, cruising taxes, fishing licenses, and crew visa status should surface as a pre departure panel. Missing items block departure or increase the chance of fines and surprise holds. This keeps the logistics role front and center and rewards planning.
Port Infrastructure and Capacity
Make marinas finite. Prime berths sell out for event weeks, forcing anchoring or second choice locations. Add beam and draft gates at certain harbors. A deep draft yacht cannot enter at low tide without a delay. Display port notes so the player learns local quirks that repeat across a season.
Crew Contracts, Rotations, and Fatigue
Replace simple day rates with contracts. Captains and key crew prefer monthly retainers with time on and time off patterns. Fatigue accumulates during continuous operations with small penalties to service and safety after long runs. Rotations reduce fatigue but create handover friction and training needs.
Example: a three week sprint with overnight passages leaves a tired chief stew who starts missing details; scheduling a relief at the next finishing port improves service and tips for the following charter.
Training Trees and Certifications
Add multi path training with visible trees. Technical track for engineers, service track for stews, tender master and water safety for deck. Certifications unlock hard gates such as night diving or helicopter operations. Training blocks the staffer and costs tuition but raises the ceiling and reduces incident rates tied to their domain.
Guests, Preferences, and Memory
Give guests simple profiles and the ability to rebook. Preferences such as quiet coves, wine service, or toys should influence satisfaction and tips. After a great week, a guest may prebook next season at a discount. A bad week should produce a negative review that suppresses top tier offers for a period.
Example: a family that loves quiet anchorages gives you a recurring August week if you avoid nightlife ports and put a kayak on the foredeck every morning.
Contracts and Legal Terms
Introduce actual charter contracts with options. Fuel inclusive or fuel excluded, APA management, minimum gratuity line, cancellation terms, and weather clauses. Each choice shifts exposure between you and the client. A tropical storm watch should test the clauses and demonstrate the value of stronger paperwork.
Multi Vessel Dispatch and Scheduling Conflicts
Scale the operations view to handle multiple active legs with a calendar timeline and conflict warnings. Double booking a captain should trigger an error before you accept. Deadhead legs should be easy to chain into profitable sequences with a preview of total transit days and costs for the chain.
Seasonal Migration and Homeport Strategy
Give seasons teeth. Move the fleet north and south on continental coasts or between basins for peak revenue. This creates windows where moving early captures pricing but risks empty weeks. Add winter maintenance slots with yard discounts that tempt you to schedule downtime at the right moment.
Marketing, CRM, and Lead Gen
Add a simple marketing panel. Spend on targeted ads or partner brokers in specific regions. Watch inquiries increase proportionally with diminishing returns. Build a CRM list of past guests and send an offer blast that fills shoulder weeks. Tie this back into the reputation system to reward consistent quality.
Competitor Operators and Market Pressure
Seed the world with AI operators. They take some of the offers you ignore and occasionally overpay for fuel or get stuck for lack of crew. When an event opens with limited slots, you will see the competition on the calendar. A rival accident in your region should briefly raise insurance costs and lower guest confidence.
Advanced Weather Routing and Oceanography
Add currents, prevailing wind roses, and tide gates for bars and narrow entrances. The planner should propose a recommended departure window that saves time and improves comfort. For river bars and canal locks, embed opening windows and real queues so a late arrival can cost an entire day.
Event Driven Scenarios and Challenges
Ship curated scenarios that teach the systems. A hurricane approach that forces an evacuate or ride out choice. A last minute Cannes Film Festival request that only pencils out if you ship the yacht and nail the berthing lottery. A remote dive expedition that pays a premium but requires new certifications and spares.
Financial Depth and Accounting
Add depreciation, resale value tied to condition and hours, and tax treatment per region. Introduce a basic ledger that clarifies gross versus net per charter. Allow refinancing or sale leaseback of a vessel mid season to free cash for expansion, with clear modeling of interest and risk.
Supply Chain and Vendor Relations
Track your relationships with local vendors. A loyal diver compressor technician gives faster service and better parts pricing after several jobs. Abuse a vendor with late payments and watch priority fade. Present vendor cards with notes and a simple star rating to nudge long term thinking.
Toys and Consumables Inventory
Represent toys as real assets with maintenance. E foils, jet skis, sea bobs, and dive compressors need periodic service and have incident risks. Consumables like oxygen for diving and premium wines renew per charter and are priced by region. Mismanaging toys should show up in Activities and cost.
Emergency Response and SAR Integration
Build a rare but consequential emergency layer. Medical events, man overboard drills, and minor fires should appear with low probability and higher likelihood when undermanned. Successful response preserves reputation and may even generate a news story that bumps demand. Failures create downtime and claims.
Story Events and Personality Hooks
Let staff personalities trigger story beats. The charming coastal captain gets invited to a local regatta and negotiates a marketing swap. The gruff passage maker offends a picky guest and needs the chief stew to repair the relationship. These moments should nudge crew mixing and hiring strategy.
Accessibility, Localization, and Modding Hooks
Add scalable fonts, high contrast mode, and keyboard navigation. Prepare the text system for localization by moving all strings to resource files. Expose data driven catalogs for vessels, ports, and events so the community can add regions and models without code changes.
Telemetry, Tuning, and Difficulty Modes
Instrument the sim to log decisions that feel punishing or confusing. Add difficulty presets that adjust demand volatility, insurance generosity, and breakdown rate. Provide a sandbox slider board where advanced players can tune realism toggles like strict waste limits or aggressive weather.
Audio and Visual Polish
Add a day and night map tint, gentle wake trails that scale with speed, and port icons that reflect size and amenities. Light touch audio improves feedback such as a soft chime on booking, a low swell on heavy weather, and a dock bell on arrival. Keep the mix subtle so players can live in it for hours.
Tutorial, Encyclopedia, and Replay
Create a short scenario based tutorial that teaches the operational loop with real consequences held in a safe bracket. Build an in game encyclopedia for vessel classes, certifications, and regulations. Add a replay of the last thirty in game days with a condensed timeline so players can diagnose mistakes.
Engineering Roadmap
Refactor toward data driven definitions for vessels, ports, events, and staff so design changes require no code edits. Introduce unit tests for core calculators such as fuel burn, tip rates, and range. Add a headless simulation harness for balance testing. Consider packaging as a progressive web app so long runs survive reloads and can be resumed on another machine with cloud saves.
Acceptance Targets For These Features
The market generates seasonally plausible offers by region and responds to weather and reputation. Vessel legs consume fuel and time according to a speed choice and sea state with bunkering and waste stops where required. Incidents name parts and create real downtime unless spares are carried. Insurance and compliance affect both cost and exposure with readable consequences. Ports feel real with capacity and constraints. Crew management introduces contracts, fatigue, training, and rotations that matter. Guests form memories that affect rebooking and reviews. The financial layer shows true profitability per charter after fuel, crew, provisions, dockage, and depreciation. The UI supports multi vessel dispatch with clear conflicts and aids decision making with a timeline and planner. The simulation produces stories that make sense in hindsight and encourage smarter planning in the next season.
Phased Delivery
Phase one focuses on performance curves, fuel and port logistics, and the dynamic market so the money loop becomes honest. Phase two layers contracts, insurance, compliance, and crew rotations so the operational reality shows up. Phase three adds incidents, spares, and seasonal migration so planning is strategic. Phase four builds guests, CRM, and competitors so demand becomes a living world. Phase five delivers polish with audio, tutorial, encyclopedia, and accessibility.
Example Player Stories These Features Unlock
A player accepts a last minute corporate event in St Barths, ships a yacht early at a premium, threads a fuel stop that barely fits draft, and beats a rival to the coveted quay. Another player stocks spares, rides a late season cold front south at nine knots to save fuel, arrives with a rested rotation, and uses a CRM discount to fill a shoulder week. A third player ignores compliance, gets held at customs for a day, misses departure, and watches a five star guest cancel, learning in a way that sticks.
This is the complete set of missing features to take the sim from clever prototype to a rich management game that rewards foresight, grit, and a little bravado.

Charter Challenge: Complete Continuation Brief For The Next Chat
One-sentence purpose
Build and iterate on a single-page web sim where the player starts with 10,000 dollars on January 1 and tries to reach 1,000,000 dollars by December 31 by buying vessels, hiring crew, routing between ports, accepting charters, managing provisions, riding weather, and making operational tradeoffs.
Current build and where to get it
The latest working bundle is v0.6. It contains index.html, style.css, and main.js. Download it and open index.html in a modern desktop browser. Save and Load use localStorage. Download: sandbox:/mnt/data/charter_challenge_v0_6.zip
Top level UI
Five panes sit on the left, a Leaflet map on the right with pulsing “beacon” markers for vessels and status. The tab set is Navigation, Weather, Fleet, Store, Staff, Assets, Overview. A header shows Date, Cash, Reputation, and Status. A time panel lets you run automatically or advance manually. The slider controls seconds per in-game day. You can jump to the next event.
Time and pacing
Auto advance is supported. One in-game day can be as fast as two tenths of a second and can be slowed to half a minute. Manual buttons add one day or skip to the next event. Interest accrues monthly, training and passages tick daily, and charters decrement per day, with a six hour Prom Night counting as one quarter day.
Map and movement
Ports: Seattle, Friday Harbor, Vancouver, Astoria, San Francisco, Sausalito, Santa Cruz, Santa Barbara, San Diego, Cabo San Lucas, Miami, Charleston, New York, St. Thomas, St. Barths, San Juan PR, Honolulu. Sailing under own power computes elapsed days from hull speed, weather multiplier, and crew speed multiplier. Shipping puts the boat on a hauler at roughly eight hundred nautical miles per day plus handling time. If the route crosses west to east or east to west, the plan inserts a one week Panama Canal notice and a surcharge. Route lines are blue for sailing, amber for shipping, green during charters. A pulsing beacon tracks each vessel’s live position along the line.
Weather visualization and buying accuracy
Weather has a historical coarse layer and an accurate fine layer. The coarse layer paints large cells in green for smooth, yellow for mixed, red for problem. Tooltips include tone-setting sailor quotes like “The winds off Point Reyes give any gale a run for her money” and “There be wild tales of mermaids and monsters in this bay.” The fine layer is tile based. Buying a five square mile tile costs fifty dollars and reveals accurate conditions in that tile. A simple brush control lets you buy multiple adjacent tiles. Fine tiles persist in your save. The selected weather state calm or moderate or rough also affects sailing risk, speed, and delays.
Fleet categories and concrete models
Categories and examples are live in the Store. Tender under thirty feet includes RIB 750 and Boston Whaler 350. Sportsman thirty to sixty feet includes Contender 32, Axopar 37, and Zeelander 55. Yacht sixty to one hundred twenty feet includes Bering 65, Nordhavn 71, Selene 66, and Bering 80. These mark explorer true where relevant. Super Yacht one hundred twenty to two hundred ninety nine feet includes a Heesen fifty meter and a Feadship sixty meter. Giga Yacht three hundred to six hundred feet includes a one hundred meter gigayacht and a five hundred passenger mini cruise ship. Each model defines speed in knots, range in nautical miles, cost per nautical mile, daily opex, guest capacity, an earning multiplier by class, length in feet, explorer true or false, and crucially the minimum, recommended, and maximum crew bands plus pantry and cold storage capacity and a galley grade.
Crew bands and gating
Every hull shows Crew Min, Rec, and Max with a meter. Below minimum blocks acceptance of a charter. Above maximum blocks as well. Between minimum and recommended is allowed but caps service. Above recommended is allowed but you pay idle payroll every idle day and you may take a small reputation and efficiency hit if guest count is low for the headcount you are carrying.
Engineers and technical rules
At eighty feet and up an engineer is required. Without one, Accept refuses. With one, in-transit incidents are less frequent and cheaper, repair costs trend down, and yard time is shorter. On very large platforms this will later expand to Chief plus Junior, and eventually an ETO role for electronics. The code already applies an engineer factor to underway incident chance and calculates tangible yard reductions.
Staff ecosystem and how pay works
Staff now includes a fixed captain roster rather than infinite clones. Each captain exposes Personality, Efficiency, and Experience and carries an explicit qualified length ceiling. There is a short quote for color and a paragraph profile. Day rate and signing bonus match profile seniority. Eligibility uses the ceiling. If the vessel length exceeds the captain’s ceiling by more than ten feet you cannot accept a charter unless you either pair the captain with an experienced mate or send the captain to Size Up training, which increases their ceiling by twenty feet and keeps them off the schedule for the training period. Non-captain roles come with salary per day and contributions to speed, safety, and service. Extra specialists like massage therapist or surf instructor are hireable for larger luxury programs and improve the Activities and Service sensations of the trip.

Staff cards and CVs
The Staff pane shows a captain gallery with avatar, quote, chips for the three ratings, ceiling, day rate, signing, a paragraph profile, and a recent vessels line. View CV opens a modal that stays in front of the map. Non-captain roles include mate, engineer, stew, massage therapist, personal trainer, surf instructor. Each shows day rate, a short narrative, and certifications. Training blocks the staffer from work and improves either ceiling for captains through Size Up or raises one of service, safety, or speed effects for non-captains.
Provisions and the Cuisine system
Provisions are now real inventory. Each hull declares pantry units, cold units, and a galley grade that shades the Cuisine sub-score. The Store has a Provisions section with a quality selector basic or standard or premium, guests and days inputs, and a one click Plan For Charter. The plan computes required units with this idea: total people equals guests plus crew and required units equal people times days times a charter class multiplier. For Luxury and VIP the multiplier is higher than one, for Fishing it is near one, for Transport it is low. Loading provisions spends cash and records loaded units, quality, and unit cost. During a charter provisions are consumed. At the end of a charter remaining provisions are wiped and any leftover is treated as spoilage loss equal to leftover units times unit cost. Cuisine is computed as a function of loaded units against required units and the charter’s cuisine expectation. Running light drops Cuisine and hurts tips and reputation. Running heavy costs money through spoilage.
Satisfaction and tip logic
Satisfaction breaks into five sub-scores. Service reflects crew attentiveness and especially captain personality. Cuisine reflects the provisioning plan and quality versus the trip’s expectation. Safety reflects incidents avoided and the general feeling of competence. Comfort reflects hull and sea state and will be refined to use weather and vessel later. Activities reflects toys and specialist staff. The tip engine uses category specific rules. Transport earns zero. Fishing tips ten percent by default and twenty percent with special toys or standout service. Luxury and Adventure tip ten percent at a minimum when you meet the brief, twenty percent for great service and cuisine, and twenty five to thirty percent for excellent across the board. The trip summary shows base, tip amount and rate, and the split is explicit.
Charter categories and starter offers
Categories are Transport, Fishing, Adventure, Luxury, VIP, and Ultra VIP. Two low reputation starter offers always spawn. Prom Night is a six hour local charter that pays roughly five to ten thousand dollars depending on vessel class and creates heavy cleaning. Transport of fishing and mining equipment pays poorly and imposes five days of required repair and cleanup. Adventure requires an explorer class yacht of sixty feet and above. Luxury expects at least two stews and an average crew rating of four point zero. VIP and Ultra VIP unlock higher service and Activities expectations and respond strongly to support vessels such as helicopters or submersibles.
Accept gating and concrete checks
Accept checks origin matching your vessel location. It checks reputation against the charter requirement. It checks engineer presence for vessels at eighty feet and above. It checks captain eligibility ceiling against vessel length, with a ten foot grace. It checks category requirements for explorer programs and stews for luxury. It checks crew bands. If any check fails a toast appears that tells you exactly what to do next such as “Hire and assign an engineer” or “Send your captain to Size Up training” or “Use Fleet then Ship to origin” or “Add a second stew.”
Wear, cleaning, repairs, and downtime
Each charter applies wear to hull condition scaled by crew safety. Cleaning days decrease with headcount and can be increased by charter type. Transport forces five days even with heavy crew. Repairs and cleaning incur cash costs based on length and purchase price. The vessel moves to the destination and flips to a Maintenance status. Yard days tick down daily and only then does the vessel return to Moored.
Over-staffing penalties that matter
Carrying more crew than the guest count and category warrant drags reputation and Efficiency. The model computes a simple needed headcount from guest count and compares it with actual. Excess headcount applies a reputation delta on completion and raises idle payroll burn when you are moored. Over-staffing is allowed, you will just feel it in money and ratings.
Store items and support vessels
Support items live in the Store. Fast Tender raises charter value and effective range. Helicopter lifts value and opens VIP offers. Fuel barge extends relocation range. Submersible and dirt bikes live under Adventure, improving VIP value and Activities. Items are added to a specific vessel, not globally, and their effects are multiplicative with the satisfaction system.
Image system for vessels and staff
The Assets tab lets you upload images for any vessel model and any staff profile. Images are stored as data URLs in the browser and override placeholders wherever they appear. The app looks up images by keys “vessel_” and “staff_”. Examples: vessel_bering65 or vessel_zeelander55 or staff_cap_jerry. If you self-host and prefer files, drop images into an assets folder and keep the same names. The app favors uploaded images and falls back to files or placeholders.
Error messages and toasts
All blocked actions raise a small toast that explains what went wrong and what to do next. Examples include insufficient cash with a suggestion to take a short charter or a bridge loan, insufficient reputation with a prompt to accept a Transport or Prom Night first, wrong origin with a pointer to Fleet Ship, captain ceiling too low with a pointer to Size Up training, and engineer missing on larger vessels with an instruction to hire and assign.
Finance and year goal
Cash starts at ten thousand dollars. Loans are available under Overview, accrue one percent monthly simple interest, and can be repaid at any time with interest paid first. The year ends on December thirty one. If cash is at or above one million dollars you hit the goal. If not, the log notes the miss without melodrama.
Code structure and anchors for the next developer
State holds the entire game state. Date, cash, reputation, loans, fleet, staff, offers, forecast data, assets, and timers live there. Catalog holds vessel definitions with crew bands and storage. Captain roster is a fixed array with the ten named captains above plus Captain Javier. Ports are predeclared with id, name, lat, lon, and region. Navigation computes route cost and time with estimateMove and activates movement with go. Offers are created with makeOfferType and generateOffers and always include Prom Night and Transport for zero reputation play. Provisions planning uses planProvisionsFor and loading uses loadProvisions. Satisfaction sub-scores are calculated with cuisineScoreFor and serviceScoreFor. Tips come from calcTipRate. Charters complete through completeCharter, which also wipes provisions and books spoilage loss. The pre-charter checklist modal offers one click Load for this charter and Accept now. The Assets manager uses base64 data URLs in localStorage keyed by model and staff identifiers. The map is Leaflet. Pulsing beacons use a simple CSS keyframes animation. All error copy flows through toast.

Save writes all game state including uploaded images and bought forecast tiles to localStorage. Load restores date, cash, reputation, fleet, staff, provisions state, forecast tiles, and image data. The Leaflet layers are reconstructed at load time from saved tiles and simple rules.
How to start a clean year quickly
Pick a start port in Overview. Click New Year which resets date and finances but keeps any uploaded images. Buy a small hull in Store, hire a captain in Staff, assign them in the roster table, flip to Navigation, accept Prom Night or Transport, and use the Provisions Plan button in Store if you want to verify the numbers before loading from the pre-charter checklist.
